  {/* MONTH 1 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Month 1 – Understand Your Money</h2>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 1: Audit Your Finances</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The first week is all about awareness. Download the last three months of your bank statements, credit card statements, UPI history, and loan repayments. Now divide every expense into three categories:</p>
  
  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li><strong className="text-gray-900">1. Fixed Expenses:</strong> House rent, home loan EMI, electricity bill, internet, insurance, school fees, groceries.</li>
    <li><strong className="text-gray-900">2. Lifestyle Expenses (Wants):</strong> Swiggy, Zomato, movies, shopping, OTT subscriptions, coffee, weekend trips.</li>
    <li><strong className="text-gray-900">3. Debt Payments:</strong> Credit card dues, personal loans, education loans, vehicle loans.</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Now calculate your monthly average. Many people discover they spend ₹5,000–₹10,000 every month on things they barely remember buying.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 2: Reduce Expenses Without Feeling Miserable</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Don't cut everything. Cut what's unnecessary. Ask yourself one question before every expense: <strong>"Will this purchase make my life better after one month?"</strong> If the answer is no, skip it.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Instead of trying to save ₹20 everywhere, focus on your biggest expenses first: negotiating rent, changing your internet plan, refinancing a loan, or cooking at home. Imagine saving ₹8,000 every month. That's nearly ₹1 lakh every year.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 3: Automate Your Savings</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Here's one habit that changed my financial life: <strong>I started paying myself first.</strong></p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The day my salary arrives, money automatically moves into savings and investments before I can spend it. Automate transfers to your Emergency Fund, SIPs, PPF, or NPS. When saving becomes automatic, discipline becomes effortless.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 4: Make a Debt Repayment Plan</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If you have high-interest credit card debt, this becomes your top priority. Paying only the minimum due keeps you trapped for years. Focus on clearing the highest-interest debt first while continuing minimum payments on the others. Every loan you close increases your future savings potential.</p>

  

  {/* MONTH 2 */}

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 5: Build Your Emergency Fund</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Your bike breaks down. A family member needs treatment. You suddenly lose your job. Life doesn't give advance notice. An emergency fund is money kept aside <em>only</em> for unexpected situations.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Don't invest your emergency fund in stocks. Keep it in a high-interest savings account, liquid mutual fund, or sweep-in FD. The purpose isn't high returns; the purpose is peace of mind.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 6: Start Investing (Don't Wait)</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">One of the biggest mistakes people make is waiting until they have "more money" before investing. The truth? <strong>Time is more valuable than money.</strong></p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For beginners in India, start with a Nifty 50 Index Fund or Flexi Cap Mutual Fund via SIP. Even ₹500 per month is enough to start. Don't underestimate small beginnings.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 7: Increase Your Income</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">There's a limit to how much you can save, but almost no limit to how much you can earn. Ask for a salary raise if you deserve it. Learn a high-income skill (Data Analysis, AI Tools, Sales). Or start a side income (freelancing, tutoring, consulting). A side income of ₹10,000–₹20,000 per month speeds up your goals massively.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 8: Set Clear Financial Goals</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Without goals, it's easy to lose motivation. Instead of saying, "I want to save money," say: "I want to save ₹2 lakh for a house down payment" or "Invest ₹15 lakh over the next 10 years." Break big dreams into monthly targets.</p>

  {/* MONTH 3 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Month 3 – Build Habits for Long-Term Wealth</h2>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 9: Use Credit Cards Wisely</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Credit cards are simply financial tools. A simple rule I personally follow: <strong>If I don't have enough money in my bank account to pay for something today, I don't buy it using a credit card.</strong> Spend only what you can repay in full, and never treat your limit as extra income.</p>

  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Week 10: Track Your Net Worth</h3>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Your salary tells you how much you earn. Your net worth tells you whether you're actually becoming wealthier. The formula is simple: <strong>Net Worth = Total Assets – Total Liabilities.</strong></p>
