  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Refined Oils and Cold-Pressed Oils: What Is Different?
  </h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">
    Extraction and refining describe different processing steps. The list below outlines the methods discussed in this comparison, including solvent extraction, neutralisation, and deodorisation. Processing varies by oil and manufacturer, so avoid assuming that every refined product follows an identical process or contains the same final composition.
  </p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li><strong>Chemical Solvent Extraction:</strong> Leftover seed pulp is washed with a petroleum derivative called Hexane to dissolve the remaining oil. While evaporated later, dangerous trace amounts often remain.</li>
    <li><strong>Acid Degumming & Neutralization:</strong> The crude oil is treated with phosphoric acid and sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) to remove natural gums. This process creates a soap-like byproduct that must be chemically washed out.</li>
    <li><strong>High-Heat Deodorization (The Killer Step):</strong> The oil is heated to extreme temperatures (reaching 230°C–260°C) to remove its natural smell and color. This extreme heat breaks down the fatty acid chains, forcing the creation of dangerous <em>trans fats</em> and <em>glycidyl esters</em>—compounds heavily linked to cardiovascular diseases, arterial blockages, and chronic body inflammation.</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">
    The article's comparison cites losses of up to 90% for Vitamin E, natural antioxidants, and polyphenols in refined oil. Actual nutrient levels depend on the source ingredient and processing; check product information rather than treating one figure as a universal value for every oil.
  </p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    How Cold-Pressed Extraction Works (Kacchi Ghani)
  </h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">
    Cold-pressed oils are produced by mechanically pressing an ingredient, using traditional wooden or metal crushers in some processes. The method relies on slower pressing and aims to limit heat from friction; the stated temperature range in this guide is below 40°C to 50°C. Manufacturing practices can differ, so a buyer who needs a specific process should check the brand's description.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">
    Compared with high-heat refining, cold pressing is intended to retain more of the ingredient's original aroma and some naturally occurring compounds. It does not by itself prove that an oil is unadulterated or nutritionally ideal. Look for clear sourcing, storage, and quality information, and choose a product whose flavour and cooking use suit your household.
  </p>
