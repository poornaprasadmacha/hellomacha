  {/* SECTION 3 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Why Rich People Sometimes Use Debt</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Wealthy individuals and business owners often have assets, cash, and investments. When they need capital to expand, they don't automatically sell their assets. Selling investments reduces ownership and liquidity. Borrowing allows them to keep assets while accessing capital.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">This does not mean borrowing is automatically better. The loan has a cost and repayment risk. The strategy only works if the borrower can manage that risk.</p>

  

  {/* SECTION 4 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Indian Tax Rules & Home Loans</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Indian tax rules can affect the economics of borrowing. For example, the Income Tax Department currently provides specific treatment for eligible housing-loan interest under <strong>Section 24(b)</strong>.</p>

  <blockquote className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-serif italic text-gray-900 border-l-4 border-red-700 pl-6 my-12 leading-relaxed">But don't make this mistake: “I get a tax benefit, so I should take a bigger loan.” The tax benefit should be considered <strong>after</strong> affordability and financial risk.</blockquote>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">An affordable loan can be useful. An unaffordable loan remains risky even when tax benefits exist. Always check the rules applicable to your particular assessment year, as India is transitioning from the Income-tax Act, 1961 to the Income-tax Act, 2025.</p>

  {/* SECTION 5 */}

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">EMIs can make expensive things look cheap. When you see an advertisement for <em>“Buy now for only ₹9,999 per month,”</em> your brain focuses on the small number.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If you combine a ₹30,000 home EMI, a ₹15,000 car EMI, an ₹8,000 personal-loan EMI, and a ₹5,000 credit-card repayment, that is <strong>₹58,000 per month</strong>. Each EMI might have looked affordable separately, but together, they destroy your financial freedom.</p>
