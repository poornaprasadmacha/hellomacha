  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Gold ETF vs. Mutual Fund SIP</h2>
  
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A Gold ETF is traded on an exchange and aims to track the domestic price of gold, after expenses and tracking differences. Buying and selling generally requires a demat and trading account, and liquidity depends on the individual ETF. A gold mutual fund typically invests in a Gold ETF and can be purchased without a demat account; it may support a scheduled SIP, but its expense structure can differ from the underlying ETF. Check scheme documents rather than assuming all funds work identically.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">A decision checklist before making your first purchase</h2>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">First decide why you want gold. Jewellery for a future occasion, a small savings habit and portfolio diversification are different goals. Digital gold may be convenient for frequent tiny amounts, while an exchange-traded product or mutual fund may be easier to review alongside other investments. Gold does not produce interest or business earnings, and its price can fall as well as rise. Avoid treating a gold purchase as a guaranteed store of value over every short period.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For digital gold, check whether the app lets you see the rate before confirming a purchase and how that rate compares with the sell-back quote. A buy-sell spread means the displayed value of your holdings may be higher than the amount available if you sell immediately. Ask how fractional ownership is documented, who the custodian is, whether the gold is insured, whether there are storage or inactivity charges, and whether physical delivery has a minimum quantity or delivery fee. Save a copy of the terms that apply when you sign up.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For a Gold ETF, check the expense ratio, tracking difference, trading volume and bid-ask spread. A low expense ratio alone is not enough if the ETF is thinly traded or the spread is wide when you place an order. Use a limit order if you understand how it works, and remember that market prices can move while an order is pending. The ETF's objective and portfolio disclosures should explain how it holds or tracks gold.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For a gold mutual fund, review the underlying scheme, total expenses and minimum investment. A SIP automates contributions but does not remove market risk or guarantee a profit. The fund may charge more than the ETF it invests in, so compare the combined cost and check redemption processing times. Read the current scheme information document and key information memorandum before investing.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Tax treatment depends on the instrument and the rules in force when you invest or redeem. The statement that profits are taxed according to an income slab for a particular holding period may not apply universally to every digital-gold or fund product, and tax rules can change. Keep purchase records, note transaction dates and confirm current tax treatment with an official source or qualified tax professional rather than relying on a generic app screen.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A ₹500 monthly comparison is most useful when the assumptions are visible. In the example on this page, the figures are illustrative and depend on the assumed gold-price movement, fees, spread and tax. They are not a clean head-to-head result for every provider. A fair comparison would use the same contribution dates and reference gold price, then deduct each product's actual purchase and exit costs and apply current taxes. Since those inputs change, use the example to notice the cost categories—not to expect a particular ending balance.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Also consider how much of your total savings should be in gold at all. A portfolio concentrated in one asset can be vulnerable to that asset's price swings. Keep cash for near-term needs and make sure essential goals are not dependent on selling gold at a favourable time. If you are unsure about your allocation, start by writing down the goal, time horizon and maximum amount you could tolerate seeing fluctuate.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A practical process is simple: identify the goal, shortlist the product, compare buy and sell prices, read the custody or scheme documents, confirm the tax rules and decide how you will exit. Review these details again if the provider changes its terms. Convenience has value, but a clear understanding of costs and ownership gives you a better basis for choosing than an attractive daily-savings animation.</p>
