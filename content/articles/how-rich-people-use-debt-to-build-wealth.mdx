  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">A Lesson I Never Learned in School</h3>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A familiar money rule in many families is, “Avoid loans.” It is understandable: a poorly sized EMI or high-cost borrowing can put pressure on an entire household. But a blanket rule misses an important distinction. Borrowing for consumption is not the same decision as borrowing to fund a business asset or education, and neither purpose makes a loan automatically safe.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">“Never take loans.”</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The useful question is not whether all debt is good or all debt is bad. It is what the borrowed money will do, how much it costs, and whether the repayment plan can survive an ordinary setback. The examples below illustrate different outcomes; they are not promises that a business purchase will succeed or that every consumer purchase is irresponsible.</p>

  <div className="bg-gray-50 border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">
    <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Consider someone taking a ₹15 lakh loan for a luxury car, largely because friends are upgrading. The car may provide comfort, but it also brings an EMI and running costs, while the outstanding loan does not disappear as the vehicle ages. If the payment crowds out savings or essentials, the purchase can become a source of pressure.</p>
    <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Another person also borrowed money.</p>
    <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Now consider borrowing to equip a small printing business. The machinery might help the owner serve more customers, but the business still needs demand, operating cash, and good execution. Revenue is not the same as profit, and profit is not guaranteed. Before borrowing, the owner needs a realistic plan for costs and repayment even if sales take time to grow.</p>
  </div>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">These examples show why the purpose of a loan matters, but they do not prove that productive borrowing always works. Business debt can fail; a home loan can be manageable or burdensome; and a vehicle can be necessary for work. The result depends on the price, the terms, the household's cash flow, and the outcome of the thing being financed.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Debt itself wasn’t the problem. The purpose behind the debt was.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A practical way to judge a loan is to write down its total cost, the cash flow it is expected to support, and what happens if that expectation is wrong. This is less exciting than calling debt “fuel,” but it is what separates a considered financing decision from wishful thinking.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">The Biggest Myth About Debt</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most people think debt is either completely good or completely bad. Reality is different.</p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li>A hammer can build a house.</li>
    <li>The same hammer can also break a window.</li>
    <li>Should we blame the hammer? Of course not.</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Debt is a contract with a cost and a schedule, not a shortcut to wealth. Used without a repayment plan, it can create stress and restrict future choices. Used to fund something productive, it may help a borrower act sooner than saving the full amount first—but only if the expected value justifies the interest and risk.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Some businesses borrow because they have a specific use for capital and a way to service the debt. A household cannot assume it has the same margin for error. A stable salary, a reserve, the loan's interest rate, and the ability to keep paying when income changes all affect whether borrowing is sensible.</p>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Why Some People Stay Poor Despite Earning Well
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A high income does not automatically produce savings. Recurring commitments, lifestyle upgrades, and unplanned spending can absorb the difference between earnings and expenses. Someone with a smaller income may build a steadier cushion by keeping fixed costs modest and saving consistently.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">At the same time, another person earning less manages to save and invest regularly.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Income alone does not create wealth. Money habits do.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people increase their lifestyle whenever their salary increases. New phone. New bike. New furniture. Bigger house. Foreign trip. Everything is purchased on EMI.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">When several EMIs arrive before the next salary, future income is already committed. Interest payments do not always mean a borrower made a bad choice—some loans may serve a real need—but borrowing for a series of upgrades can leave little room for emergencies or long-term goals. Before adding another EMI, total the payments you already owe.</p>

    <div className="md:col-span-3">

      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Friend A</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Spends the bonus on a premium bike, a new smartphone, and an expensive watch. Those items may be enjoyable, but they generally do not create a regular income stream for the buyer.</p>
      <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">After three years: The bike is worth less. The phone is outdated. The watch has little resale value. His money is gone.</p>

      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Friend B</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Uses the same ₹5 lakh for training, equipment, and a small online business. That plan could improve earning potential, but results are uncertain; the person should validate demand and avoid treating the entire amount as guaranteed to grow.</p>
      <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Three years later: The business earns income every month. The investment keeps working. His money continues growing.</p>

      <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">The amount invested was identical. The result was completely different.</p>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Rich People Ask a Different Question
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most people ask, <em>“Can I afford this EMI?”</em></p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Successful investors ask, <em>“Will this purchase generate more money than it costs?”</em></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The question helps make the trade-off visible, but it is not a guarantee. An asset can lose value or fail to earn; some purchases that do not produce income may still serve an important family need. Estimate expected income conservatively, include upkeep and taxes where relevant, and compare the result with the loan's full repayment cost before deciding.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Good Debt vs Bad Debt</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people think all loans belong in one category. They don’t.</p>

  <div className="my-8 overflow-x-auto">
    <table className="w-full text-left text-base border-collapse">
      <thead>
        <tr>
          <th className=" border-gray-900 py-3 px-4 font-bold text-gray-900 bg-gray-50 w-1/2">Good Debt ✅</th>
          <th className=" border-gray-900 py-3 px-4 font-bold text-gray-900 bg-gray-50 w-1/2">Bad Debt ❌</th>
        </tr>
      </thead>
      <tbody className="text-gray-800">
        <tr className="hover:bg-red-50  border-gray-100"><td className="py-4 px-4">Creates income</td><td className="py-4 px-4">Creates expenses</td></tr>
        <tr className="hover:bg-red-50  border-gray-100"><td className="py-4 px-4">Builds assets</td><td className="py-4 px-4">Buys lifestyle</td></tr>
        <tr className="hover:bg-red-50  border-gray-100"><td className="py-4 px-4">Increases future earning</td><td className="py-4 px-4">Reduces future income</td></tr>
        <tr className="hover:bg-red-50  border-gray-100"><td className="py-4 px-4">Helps wealth creation</td><td className="py-4 px-4">Creates financial pressure</td></tr>
        <tr className="hover:bg-red-50  border-gray-100"><td className="py-4 px-4">Has a clear repayment plan</td><td className="py-4 px-4">Often emotional spending</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">The label “good debt” is shorthand, not a stamp of approval. A loan for a productive asset can still be too expensive or too large. A loan for a home or vehicle can still be reasonable if the need, repayment, and risks fit the borrower's situation.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">A Real Example from Everyday Life</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Suppose you take a ₹10 lakh loan.</p>

  <div className="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-8 mb-16">
    <div className="bg-gray-50 border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Situation One</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">You buy a luxury SUV. Every month you pay EMI, insurance, fuel, and maintenance.</p>
      <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Result: The vehicle keeps losing value. The expenses continue.</p>
    </div>
    <div className="bg-gray-50 border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Situation Two</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">You use ₹10 lakh to open a profitable dairy business. The business generates income, which pays the loan.</p>
      <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Result: The loan ends. The business continues. The debt disappears. The asset remains.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Why Banks Love People Who Spend Emotionally
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Think about advertisements. “Zero down payment.” “Instant approval.” “Easy EMI.” “Buy now, pay later.”</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Notice what they rarely ask: <em>“Will this purchase improve your financial future?”</em></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">An offer that makes the monthly payment look small can distract from the total amount payable and the length of the commitment. Pause before signing: compare the cash price, total repayment, fees, and what the EMI would displace in your monthly budget. A promotion is not a saving if it leads you to borrow for something you did not plan to buy.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">That is why learning personal finance is one of the highest-return investments you can make.</p>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Money Should Buy Freedom, Not Pressure
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people <em>dream of becoming rich.</em> But what they actually want is freedom. Freedom to choose work. Freedom to spend time with family. Freedom from constant financial stress.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Ironically, many purchases made in the name of success reduce that freedom. A bigger EMI means less flexibility. Higher expenses mean more pressure. More pressure often means fewer choices.</p>

  <blockquote className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-serif italic text-gray-900 border-l-4 border-red-700 pl-6 my-12 leading-relaxed">Financial freedom comes from owning assets—not from owning expensive liabilities.</blockquote>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    One Simple Rule I Follow
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Before buying anything costly, ask yourself: <strong><em>Will this item pay me back?</em></strong></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If the answer is no, think twice. Sometimes spending is necessary. Sometimes enjoyment is important. But borrowing for consumption should never become a habit. Borrowing to build productive assets is a completely different conversation.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">The goal is not to avoid debt. The goal is to avoid bad decisions.</p>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    Why Rich People Don’t Hate Debt
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">One thing surprised me when I started reading about successful businesses. <strong>Almost every large company has borrowed money at some point.</strong> Big factories are built using loans. Hotels are built using loans. Airlines use loans. Even many billion-dollar companies raise debt instead of spending all their own money.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">So if debt is always bad, why do financially successful people still use it?</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Because they don’t see debt as money. They see it as <strong>fuel</strong>. A car without fuel cannot move. But pouring fuel on the road doesn’t take you anywhere either. The fuel must go into the engine.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Similarly, borrowed money should go into something that produces value.</p>

  <h1 className="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-black text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">
    The Real Enemy Is Financial Ignorance
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many families don’t become poor because they earn less. They become poor because nobody taught them how money works. Parents tell children: “Study hard.” “Get a good job.” “Save money.”</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">All good advice. But very few people explain:</p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li>How interest works</li>
    <li>How investments grow</li>
    <li>How inflation reduces purchasing power</li>
    <li>How assets create wealth</li>
    <li>How liabilities consume wealth</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-normal">Without this knowledge, people spend twenty years increasing their income and forty years paying for their past decisions.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">The Difference Between an Asset and a Liability</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">This is one of the simplest concepts in finance, yet it changes everything. An <strong>asset</strong> puts money into your pocket. A <strong>liability</strong> takes money out of your pocket.</p>

    <div className="bg-gray-50 border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">

        <li>2. Spend less than you earn.</li>

        <li>4. Invest wisely.</li>
        <li>5. Repeat for many years.</li>
      </ul>

    The 7 Money Mistakes That Keep Middle-Class Families Stuck

      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most luxury purchases provide happiness for a few weeks, but the EMI remains for years. Your bank balance doesn’t care how many likes your Instagram post received.</p>

      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">2. Thinking Salary Equals Wealth</h3>

    </div>
    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">3. Not Having an Emergency Fund</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Without savings, every emergency becomes another loan. Try building an emergency fund that covers at least six months of essential expenses.</p>
    </div>

      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">4. Waiting for the “Perfect Time”</h3>

      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Money affects almost every decision in life. Learning about budgeting, investing, taxes, and retirement is not optional. It’s essential.</p>
