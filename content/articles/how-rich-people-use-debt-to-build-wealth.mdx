  <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">A Lesson I Never Learned in School</h3>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">When I was growing up, there was one money lesson that almost every elder repeated.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">“Never take loans.”</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">It didn’t matter whether the discussion was about education, business, or buying a house. The answer was always the same. <em>“Debt will destroy your life.”</em> For many years, I believed it. Then I started observing people around me.</p>

  <div className="bg-gray-50 border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">
    <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">One person borrowed ₹15 lakh to buy a luxury car because everyone in his circle was upgrading. The excitement lasted only a few months. Soon, the EMI became a monthly headache. The car lost value every year, but the loan remained.</p>
    <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">Another person also borrowed money.</p>
    <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">But instead of buying a luxury item, he opened a small printing business. The machines he purchased started generating income from the very first month. Slowly, that same business paid his loan, supported his family, and eventually created profits.</p>
  </div>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Both people borrowed money. One became financially stressed. The other became financially stronger. At that moment, I realized something important.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">Debt itself wasn’t the problem. The purpose behind the debt was.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Unfortunately, schools teach us mathematics, science, and history, but very few teach us how money actually works. That is why many people spend their entire lives working for money instead of making money work for them.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">The Biggest Myth About Debt</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most people think debt is either completely good or completely bad. Reality is different.</p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li>A hammer can build a house.</li>
    <li>The same hammer can also break a window.</li>
    <li>Should we blame the hammer? Of course not.</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The result depends on how it is used. Debt works exactly the same way. Used <em>without planning</em>, it can create stress, anxiety, and financial problems. Used <em>wisely</em>, it can become one of the strongest tools for building wealth.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">That is why many successful entrepreneurs borrow money while many ordinary families struggle because of loans. The difference is not the loan. The difference is the strategy.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people increase their lifestyle whenever their salary increases. New phone. New bike. New furniture. Bigger house. Foreign trip. Everything is purchased on EMI.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Soon, most of their future income already belongs to someone else. They are working hard but building someone else’s wealth through interest payments.</p>

    Rich People Ask a Different Question
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most people ask, <em>“Can I afford this EMI?”</em></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">That single question changes everything. If something creates income, it may become an asset. If it only creates expenses, it is usually a liability. Understanding this difference is one of the biggest steps toward financial freedom.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">That is why learning personal finance is one of the highest-return investments you can make.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people <em>dream of becoming rich.</em> But what they actually want is freedom. Freedom to choose work. Freedom to spend time with family. Freedom from constant financial stress.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Ironically, many purchases made in the name of success reduce that freedom. A bigger EMI means less flexibility. Higher expenses mean more pressure. More pressure often means fewer choices.</p>

  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If the answer is no, think twice. Sometimes spending is necessary. Sometimes enjoyment is important. But borrowing for consumption should never become a habit. Borrowing to build productive assets is a completely different conversation.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">The goal is not to avoid debt. The goal is to avoid bad decisions.</p>

    Why Rich People Don’t Hate Debt
  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">One thing surprised me when I started reading about successful businesses. <strong>Almost every large company has borrowed money at some point.</strong> Big factories are built using loans. Hotels are built using loans. Airlines use loans. Even many billion-dollar companies raise debt instead of spending all their own money.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">So if debt is always bad, why do financially successful people still use it?</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Because they don’t see debt as money. They see it as <strong>fuel</strong>. A car without fuel cannot move. But pouring fuel on the road doesn’t take you anywhere either. The fuel must go into the engine.</p>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">Similarly, borrowed money should go into something that produces value.</p>

  </h1>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many families don’t become poor because they earn less. They become poor because nobody taught them how money works. Parents tell children: “Study hard.” “Get a good job.” “Save money.”</p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li>How interest works</li>
    <li>How investments grow</li>
    <li>How inflation reduces purchasing power</li>
    <li>How assets create wealth</li>
    <li>How liabilities consume wealth</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-8 text-lg text-gray-900 font-bold">Without this knowledge, people spend twenty years increasing their income and forty years paying for their past decisions.</p>
