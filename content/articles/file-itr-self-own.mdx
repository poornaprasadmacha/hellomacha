## **What Is an ITR?**

ITR stands for <strong>Income Tax Return</strong>.

It is the return through which a taxpayer reports relevant income, deductions, taxes paid and other required information to the Income Tax Department.

Depending on your circumstances, filing an ITR may be:

- legally required;
- useful for maintaining financial records;
- helpful when applying for certain loans;
- useful for visa or financial documentation;
- important for claiming a tax refund;
- necessary for reporting particular types of income.

The important point is simple: <strong>ITR filing is not only about salary.</strong>

Your taxable income can come from several sources.

## **FY 2025-26 vs AY 2026-27**

Before you start filing, understand the difference between Financial Year and Assessment Year.

### **What Is a Financial Year?**

The Financial Year is the period during which you earn your income.

For this return:

**FY 2025-26 = 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026**

### **What Is an Assessment Year?**

The Assessment Year is the year in which that income is assessed and reported through the income tax return.

### **ITR-1 Eligibility: Important Beginner Check**

ITR-1 can generally apply to an eligible resident individual whose total income does not exceed ₹50 lakh and whose income falls within the prescribed categories.

However, several exclusions apply.

Certain capital gains, foreign assets or income, unlisted equity shares, directorship in certain companies and other specified circumstances can affect eligibility.

### **Step 11: Report Income From Other Sources**

One of the most common beginner mistakes is reporting salary while forgetting other income.

Depending on your circumstances, other income may include:

- savings-bank interest;
- fixed-deposit interest;
- recurring-deposit interest;
- dividends;
- family pension;
- certain other taxable receipts.

#### **Example**

Suppose you earned:

- ₹9,00,000 salary;
- ₹18,000 savings interest;
- ₹32,000 FD interest;
- ₹8,000 dividends.

Do not assume that only your salary matters.

The additional income should also be reviewed for taxability and reporting.

## **ITR Deadline, Late Filing Fee, Interest and Refund Timeline for AY 2026-27**

Filing correctly is important, but timing matters too. If you are preparing your ITR for AY 2026-27, you should know the regular deadline, the belated-return deadline, the possible late-filing fee, interest provisions and what to expect after requesting a refund.

### **What Was the ITR Due Date for AY 2026-27?**

For most individual taxpayers who were **not required to get their accounts audited**, the extended due date for filing the return for **AY 2026-27 was 31 August 2026**.

AY 2026-27 relates to income earned during **FY 2025-26**, from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.

The applicable deadline can differ for taxpayers covered by audit or other special provisions, so do not assume that one date applies to every taxpayer.

### **Can I File ITR After the Due Date?**

Yes. Missing the regular due date does not automatically mean that you can no longer file your return.

For AY 2026-27, a <strong>belated return</strong> may generally be furnished on or before <strong>31 December 2026</strong>, or before completion of assessment, whichever is earlier, subject to the applicable provisions.

If you have missed the deadline, filing sooner is generally better than waiting until the last available date.

### **What Is the Late Filing Fee Under Section 234F?**

If you file a belated return, a late-filing fee under Section 234F may apply.

| Total Income | Late-Filing Fee |
|---|---:|
| Up to ₹5 lakh | ₹1,000 |
| More than ₹5 lakh | ₹5,000 |

This is a <strong>fee for delayed filing</strong>. It is separate from any income tax that may still be payable and separate from applicable interest.

### **Can Interest Also Apply When I File Late?**

Yes. A late return can involve more than the Section 234F fee.

For AY 2026-27, the return relates to income earned during FY 2025-26, so the applicable interest provisions for that tax year are governed by the relevant provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

The main provisions beginners should know are:

### **Why Can an ITR Refund Take Longer?**

There is no single official “backlog number” that should be used as a blanket promise for every taxpayer.

Do not assume that a delayed refund automatically means your return is rejected.

### **What Is a Revised Return?**

A <strong>revised return</strong> is generally used when you discover an error or omission in an ITR that you have already filed and the law still allows you to revise it.

Examples include:

- forgotten bank interest;
- omitted dividend income;
- incorrect income figures;
- incorrect deduction information;
- other mistakes in the originally filed return.

For <strong>AY 2026-27</strong>, the revised-return deadline has been extended to <strong>31 March 2027</strong>, or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier, subject to the applicable provisions.

### **Is There an Additional Fee for a Revised Return?**

Yes.

For AY 2026-27, if a revised return is filed after <strong>31 December 2026</strong> and up to <strong>31 March 2027</strong>, an additional fee under Section 234I can apply.

The fee structure is:

| Total Income | Additional Revised-Return Fee |
|---|---:|
| Up to ₹5 lakh | ₹1,000 |
| More than ₹5 lakh | ₹5,000 |

If you discover an error, it is generally better to review and correct it sooner rather than waiting for the final part of the revision window.

### **What Is ITR Rectification?**

Rectification is different from a revised return.

A revised return is generally about correcting the return you filed.

A rectification request is generally used when an eligible <strong>mistake apparent from the record</strong> remains in a processed return or applicable order/intimation.

For example, if the processing result does not correctly reflect an eligible tax credit already available in the records, rectification may be relevant.

| Situation | Usually Consider |
|---|---|
| You forgot to report bank interest | Revised Return |
| You omitted dividend income | Revised Return |
| You entered incorrect income information | Revised Return |
| You claimed something incorrectly and revision is still available | Revised Return |

| A tax credit was not correctly considered in processing | Check Rectification eligibility |

Do not use rectification simply because it sounds like a quicker way to change your return. The appropriate route depends on what went wrong and whether the return has already been processed.

### **What About an Updated Return (ITR-U)?**

An updated return is a separate mechanism from a belated return, revised return and rectification.

It has its own eligibility conditions, time limits and additional-tax consequences. It should not be treated as a routine replacement for filing a normal or revised return.

If you are considering ITR-U for AY 2026-27 or an older year, check the current eligibility rules carefully before using it.

## **What Happens After You File Your ITR?**

After submission and e-Verification, your return can move through processing.

Depending on your circumstances, you may:

- receive an intimation;
- receive a refund;
- have your return processed without further action;
- receive a request for additional information;
- receive another tax communication.

Refund processing starts after e-Verification, and the Income Tax Department says refunds usually take around **4–5 weeks** to be credited. That is a general indication, not a guaranteed timeline.

Keep checking your registered communication channels.

If you receive an official tax notice or communication, do not ignore it.

## **Can You Correct an ITR After Filing?**

Yes, but the correct correction method depends on the stage of your return and the nature of the mistake.

For AY 2026-27:

- use a **revised return** when you need to correct your own filed return and the revision window is available;
- consider **rectification** when an eligible mistake apparent from the record remains after processing;
- consider **ITR-U** only when its separate conditions and additional-tax rules apply.

For AY 2026-27, the revised-return window generally extends up to **31 March 2027**, or completion of assessment, whichever is earlier.

## **Should You File ITR If Your Tax Is Zero?**

A zero tax liability does not automatically mean that you never need to file an ITR.

The filing requirement can depend on:

- total income;
- applicable legal conditions;
- specific transactions;
- other circumstances.

Therefore, do not use:

**"My tax is zero, so I don't need to file."**

as a universal rule.

## **Why Is ITR Filing Useful?**

An Income Tax Return can provide an official record of your reported income and tax position.

Depending on the situation, your ITR records may be useful for:

- loan applications;
- visa applications;
- income documentation;
- financial planning;
- investment records;
- tax compliance;
- refund claims.

Keeping your returns organized can make future financial tasks easier.
