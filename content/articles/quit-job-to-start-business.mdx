    <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">"Welcome to entrepreneurship."</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">It feels exciting. It feels brave. It feels like the beginning of financial freedom. But what social media doesn't show you is what happens <strong>six months later</strong>.</p>

  <ul className="list-disc list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li>The savings slowly disappear.</li>
    <li>The excitement turns into stress.</li>

    <li>Customers don't magically appear.</li>
    <li>Family members start asking uncomfortable questions.</li>
  </ul>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">And many people quietly return to a job—or worse, they give up on entrepreneurship completely. You rarely see that post on LinkedIn. Because failure is much quieter than success.</p>

 

  {/* SECTION 1 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">The Biggest Lie About Entrepreneurship</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">I know this because I've asked myself the same question many times. Should I quit my corporate job and work on my startup full-time? If you know me, I'm <strong>SRK Macha</strong>, founder of <strong>HelloMacha.com</strong>, and someone who genuinely believes entrepreneurship can change lives. At the same time, I also work a full-time corporate job.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For a long time, I wondered if I was making a mistake by not resigning. Then I started reading stories from founders around the world. After reading dozens of experiences, one realization completely changed how I think about entrepreneurship:</p>

  <blockquote className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-serif italic text-gray-900 border-l-4 border-red-700 pl-6 my-12 leading-relaxed">Most successful founders don't quit because they're tired of their jobs. They quit because their businesses become impossible to run as side projects.</blockquote>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">That sentence changed everything for me. Because if I'm completely honest… if I had quit my job too early, <strong>I probably would have quit my startup too.</strong> Not because my dream wasn't real. But because financial pressure destroys patience.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">When your savings are disappearing every month, you stop thinking like a builder and start thinking like someone trying to survive. Eventually, many give up—not because the startup couldn't succeed, but because they ran out of time, money, or emotional energy.</p>

  {/* SECTION 2 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Escaping a Job Is Not the Same as Building a Business</h2>

  <img 
    alt="Social media myths about quitting a job to build a startup" 
    className="w-full h-auto object-cover border border-gray-100 my-10"
  />

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Someone has a difficult manager, low salary, or feels unappreciated. Then they conclude: <em>"I'll quit my job and start a business."</em></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">But think about it. A business doesn't remove problems. It changes the type of problems you face. Instead of one manager… You now answer to customers. Investors. Vendors. Employees. Government regulations. Cash flow. Taxes.</p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">In a job, if one customer is unhappy, someone else usually handles it. As a founder, <strong>you</strong> handle it. At 10 PM. On Sunday. Entrepreneurship doesn't remove responsibility. It multiplies it.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">"Be Your Own Boss" Sounds Great—Until You Realize You Have Hundreds of Bosses</h2>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">As an entrepreneur, you don't have fewer bosses. You have more. Your customers become your boss. If they stop buying, your revenue disappears. Your employees become your responsibility. The market decides whether your product survives.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Ironically, the only person you can't blame anymore is your manager. Everything comes back to you. That's not freedom. That's ownership.</p>

  {/* SECTION 3 */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">What Successful Entrepreneurs Did Before They Quit</h2>

  <img 
    alt="Build startup before quitting your job" 
    className="w-full h-auto object-cover border border-gray-100 my-10"
  />

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">After reading stories from founders across the globe, I noticed something fascinating. Although their businesses were different, <strong>their decision-making process was almost identical.</strong> None of them woke up one morning and said, <em>"I'm quitting my job because I have a brilliant idea."</em></p>

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Instead, they treated entrepreneurship like an investment. They reduced risk before increasing it. Let's look at what they actually did.</p>

  <div className="space-y-12">
    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Lesson 1: They Didn't Quit Because They Hated Their Jobs</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Many people quit to escape. Successful founders usually quit to expand. They left because their startup had reached a point where their job was slowing its growth. Evidence won over emotion.</p>
    </div>

    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Lesson 2: They Validated Before They Resigned</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Most people quit first, then start building. That's incredibly risky. Validation means proving that real people are willing to pay for what you're building. Money is validation. Compliments are not.</p>
    </div>

    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Lesson 3: Every Founder Talked About Financial Runway</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Money buys something far more valuable than comfort. <strong>It buys time.</strong> Without savings, every month becomes a countdown. Instead of asking how to build a great company, you start asking how to pay next month's rent.</p>
    </div>

    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Lesson 4: They Used Their Jobs as Startup Capital</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Your salary can pay for your website, software, and marketing experiments. More importantly, it pays your personal expenses so your startup doesn't have to. Your salary may be the first investment your startup ever receives.</p>
    </div>

    <div>
      <h3 className="text-xl md:text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 tracking-tight !no-underline">Lesson 5: They Built Discipline, Not Just Motivation</h3>
      <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Motivation starts businesses. Discipline builds them. The entrepreneurs who succeed are often the ones who keep showing up when they are exhausted after work. Consistency quietly beats excitement.</p>
    </div>
  </div>

  

  {/* SECTION 4: THE CHECKLIST */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">10 Signs You're NOT Ready to Quit Your Job Yet</h2>

  <img 
    alt="Startup readiness checklist" 
    className="w-full h-auto object-cover border border-gray-100 my-10"
  />

  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If you've already decided to resign, this section may make you uncomfortable. But the entrepreneurs who succeed are usually the most prepared. If several of these describe you, your next step isn't quitting your job—it's strengthening your startup.</p>

  <ol className="list-decimal list-inside space-y-3 mb-8 text-lg text-gray-800">
    <li><strong>You Don't Have a Paying Customer Yet:</strong> Ideas don't pay salaries. Customers do. Focus on getting your first paying customer before handing in your notice.</li>
    <li><strong>Your Startup Depends Entirely on Hope:</strong> Replace "People will definitely buy" with evidence like repeat customers and actual revenue.</li>
    <li><strong>You Have Less Than Six Months of Savings:</strong> If you can't survive six months without income, your startup isn't your biggest risk—running out of money is.</li>
    <li><strong>You're Trying to Escape Your Job:</strong> A bad manager is a reason to change jobs, not necessarily to start a company.</li>
    <li><strong>Your Family Doesn't Know Your Financial Reality:</strong> Great businesses are easier to build when expectations at home are clear.</li>
    <li><strong>You Think More Time Will Solve Everything:</strong> If customers aren't buying today, having 40 extra free hours a week won't magically change that. Skills are your bottleneck, not time.</li>
    <li><strong>You're Still Changing Your Idea Every Month:</strong> Constantly pivoting means you haven't committed long enough to learn.</li>
    <li><strong>You Haven't Learned How to Sell:</strong> A simple product sold effectively beats a perfect product nobody knows exists.</li>
    <li><strong>Your Startup Can't Survive Without You Working Every Hour:</strong> That's not a business; that's self-employment without scalability.</li>
    <li><strong>You Don't Have a Backup Plan:</strong> Having a backup plan doesn't make you less committed. It makes you more responsible.</li>
  </ol>
