  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700 text-base md:text-lg">

  </div>

  {/* THE CORE RULE SUMMARY BOX */}
  <div className="bg-white border border-gray-200 p-6 my-8">
    <h3 className="text-lg font-bold text-gray-900 uppercase tracking-wider mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
      The 5-4-3-2-1 Rule at a Glance
    </h3>
    <ul className="space-y-3 text-gray-700 font-medium">
      <li className="flex items-start">
        <span className="font-bold text-blue-700 w-8">5 =</span>
        <span>5+ years of fund history</span>
      </li>
      <li className="flex items-start">
        <span className="font-bold text-blue-700 w-8">4 =</span>
        <span>Beat the relevant benchmark in at least 4 of the last 5 years</span>
      </li>
      <li className="flex items-start">
        <span className="font-bold text-blue-700 w-8">3 =</span>
        <span>Rank in the top 25% (top-quartile) at least 3 times</span>
      </li>
      <li className="flex items-start">
        <span className="font-bold text-blue-700 w-8">2 =</span>
        <span>Maximum 2 funds in the same category (avoid overlap)</span>
      </li>
      <li className="flex items-start">
        <span className="font-bold text-blue-700 w-8">1 =</span>
        <span>Prefer a stable fund manager with 3+ years of experience</span>
      </li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    This is not an official investment rule or a guarantee of returns. Think of it as a quick screening checklist that can help you shortlist funds before doing deeper research.
  </div>

  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    SEBI also advises investors to consider factors such as past performance, diversification, asset allocation, tax implications, risk, and regular portfolio reviews rather than relying on one performance number.
  </div>

  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    How the 5-4-3-2-1 Rule Helps Find a Better Mutual Fund
  </h2>

  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    The 5-4-3-2-1 rule is a simple way to filter mutual funds using five basic checks. Instead of asking, <em>“Which mutual fund gave the highest return?”</em>, ask:
  </div>

  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 mb-6 text-gray-700">
    <li>Does the fund have enough history?</li>
    <li>Has it performed well against its benchmark?</li>
    <li>Has it shown consistency?</li>
    <li>Am I buying too many similar funds?</li>
    <li>Does the fund have stable management?</li>
  </ul>

  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    This approach can be useful for Indian investors building investments for goals such as retirement, children’s education, a home, or long-term wealth creation. However, no checklist can identify the best mutual fund with certainty. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risk, and past performance does not guarantee future performance.
  </div>

  {/* BREAKDOWN OF THE RULES */}
  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    5: Look for 5+ Years of History
  </h2>
  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    The first filter is simple: look for funds with at least five years of history when your goal is to evaluate long-term consistency.
  </div>
  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    A longer history gives you more information about how a fund behaved across different market conditions. You can study its performance during strong markets as well as periods of weakness.
  </div>

  <div className="mb-4 font-semibold text-gray-900">A five-year history can help you investigate:</div>
  <ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 mb-6 text-gray-700">
    <li>Performance across different market conditions</li>
    <li>Returns over multiple periods</li>
    <li>Changes in the portfolio and fund management</li>
    <li>Consistency against the benchmark</li>
    <li>Risk and volatility</li>
  </ul>

  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    4: Check Performance Against the Benchmark
  </h2>
  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    The second number in the rule is 4. The idea is to check whether the fund has beaten its relevant benchmark in at least four of the last five years, rather than celebrating one exceptional year.
  </div>

  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    In this example, the fund beat its benchmark in four out of five years. That is far more informative than simply saying, “The fund returned 17% last year.”
  </div>

  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    3: Look for Top-Quartile Consistency
  </h2>
  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    The third number suggests looking for funds that have ranked in the top 25% (top quartile) of their category at least three times during the 5-year period.
  </div>
  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    Suppose Fund A gives 35% in one year but performs poorly in several other years. Fund B gives more moderate returns but remains competitive over multiple periods. Chasing Fund A because of one spectacular year often leads to regret. Look for consistent performers instead.
  </div>

  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    2: Keep Funds Limited Within a Category
  </h2>
  <div className="mb-4 text-gray-700">
    Avoid collecting too many mutual funds that invest in similar areas. Having 10 or 15 mutual funds does not automatically mean you are well diversified.
  </div>
  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    If you own four large-cap funds, their portfolios likely contain many of the same stocks. This overlap adds unnecessary complexity without reducing risk. Limiting yourself to a maximum of 2 funds per category keeps your portfolio clean and actionable.
  </div>

  <h2 className="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-12 mb-4 border-b border-gray-200 pb-2">
    1: Check Fund Manager Stability
  </h2>
  <div className="mb-6 text-gray-700">
    Check whether the fund has a relatively stable fund manager with meaningful experience (such as 3+ years managing the specific scheme). Active funds depend heavily on the decisions of the fund management team; frequent manager turnover can change a fund's risk profile unexpectedly.
  </div>
