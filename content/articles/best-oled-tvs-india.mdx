  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">A final pre-purchase check</h2>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">If possible, view the TV in person using content you know well. Store demo loops are designed to look striking and may not reveal how ordinary cable channels, subtitles or a dimly lit film will appear at home. Ask to see a familiar scene, check the picture in a mode you would actually use, and sit at roughly your usual viewing distance. The most vivid preset is not necessarily the most comfortable for everyday viewing.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A 4K display can show detailed native 4K sources, but the source quality still matters. Compressed streams, older shows and live broadcasts can look different from a high-bitrate demo. If most of your viewing comes from a set-top box, make that part of the comparison. Confirm that the TV has enough ports for your set-top box, game console, sound system and any other device you plan to connect, and check which ports support the required features.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Don't treat advertised peak brightness as a direct measure of how the whole picture will look. HDR performance depends on the content, display settings, tone mapping and the panel's ability to maintain highlights. A set can make bright highlights pop but still handle reflections or dark detail differently from another model. Reviews and demonstrations should be judged against your room, not just a specification sheet.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">After comparing picture, sound and connectivity, compare what the purchase includes. Verify whether installation is included, if wall mounting costs extra, and how damaged-on-arrival or replacement claims work with the seller. Confirm the exact product code on the invoice and retain purchase records. Promotional card terms and product availability are time-sensitive; proceed only when the final price and conditions are clear.</p>

  <h2 className="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900 mt-16 mb-6 tracking-tight !no-underline">Choose by room, viewing distance and content</h2>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">A premium TV is only an upgrade if it fits the way your household watches. Measure the wall or cabinet before comparing screen sizes, and leave enough room for the stand, ventilation and cables. Sitting close to a very large screen can make lower-resolution cable channels look rougher, while sitting far away can make the extra detail of 4K less noticeable. Think about the usual seating distance and whether everyone watches from directly in front or from the sides.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">OLED is especially compelling for films and series watched in a dim room, where dark scenes and contrast matter. Individual pixels can switch off, so bright subtitles or stars stand apart from a dark background. That advantage is less dramatic in a bright living room, where reflections and overall brightness become more important. If your family watches news channels with a fixed logo for many hours or uses the TV as a desktop display, review the manufacturer's guidance on static images and screen-care features. OLED use does not mean burn-in is inevitable, but persistent static content is a consideration.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">The product list includes several QLED and LED sets rather than OLED panels. These televisions use a backlight, and picture performance depends on more than the panel label: local-dimming capability, peak brightness, reflection handling, viewing angle and image processing can all matter. A strong LCD can be a sensible choice for daytime sport and bright rooms, while an OLED can be a better match for contrast-focused evening viewing. Compare the specific model and return terms instead of assuming that a technology name alone determines picture quality.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">For gaming, confirm the details you actually need. A high refresh-rate panel, variable refresh rate and auto low-latency mode can make compatible games feel more responsive, but a model's advertised feature may depend on a particular HDMI input, resolution or settings menu. Check the exact console or PC connection requirements and read the TV's input specifications. If you play casually, a smooth interface, low input lag in game mode and dependable app support may matter more than a headline refresh-rate number.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Audio deserves its own check. A television's built-in speakers may be adequate for dialogue in a small room, but speaker wattage does not tell you how clear voices will sound from your seating position. Listen to speech if you can, check whether dialogue-enhancement settings are available, and make sure a soundbar can connect through the ports you plan to use. Dolby Atmos support is a format feature; it does not guarantee that the TV's built-in speakers will create a convincing surround effect.</p>
  <p className="mb-6 text-lg text-gray-800 leading-relaxed">Before ordering, verify the seller, delivery and installation terms, warranty coverage, exact model suffix, included stand and return policy. Prices and bank offers can change, and a linked listing may show a different configuration or temporary offer later. If the TV is going on a wall, check the VESA mounting pattern and whether the wall can support the set. Keep the carton until installation is complete and inspect the panel for damage before signing off. These simple checks are more useful than buying in a hurry because a sale banner says the deal ends today.</p>
